What Are QC Photos and Why They Matter When Buying a Super Clone

July 10, 2026 · 3 min read

One of the most important practices in the super clone market is Quality Control inspection, commonly called QC. If a seller does not offer QC photos before shipping, that is a serious warning sign. This guide explains what QC photos are, what to look for, and why Max Timepieces considers them non-negotiable.

What Are QC Photos?

QC photos are real photographs of your specific watch taken after the piece passes an in-house inspection. They are not stock photos, catalogue images, or reused photos from previous orders. They are taken with the actual piece you will receive, typically under bright white or natural lighting to capture dial detail, finishing, and alignment accurately.

A complete QC photo set should include: front face (dial, hands, bezel), side profile (case thickness, crown position, crown guards), caseback (engravings, material callouts), bracelet (link finishing, clasp mechanism), close-up of the date window and cyclops (if applicable), and close-up of the bezel edge and insert.

What to Check in QC Photos

**Dial:** Is the text crisp and centered? Are the applied hour markers straight and evenly spaced? Is the lume compound filled flush without overflow? Does the date magnification appear centered in the Cyclops?

**Bezel:** Is the insert centered with no gaps around the edge? Do the numerals align at 12 o'clock? Is the ceramic color uniform?

**Case:** Are the brushed and polished surfaces correctly applied (no polish bleeding onto brushed flanks)? Do the lugs taper symmetrically?

**Bracelet:** Are the links smooth with no sharp edges? Does the clasp deploy with firm tension?

Why Max Timepieces Never Ships Without QC Approval

We implemented mandatory QC photo approval because early in our history we received a complaint that a piece shipped with a slightly misaligned bezel insert. That single complaint changed our policy permanently. Today, every watch is photographed under a daylight lamp and sent to the buyer before we even pull the DHL label. You approve or we do not ship. There is no exception.

How to Read QC Photos Like a Pro

The most common issues caught in QC: slightly off-center dial logo printing, uneven lume fill in one index, bezel insert not fully seated (visible gap at one side), bracelet link that has a rough edge, and date wheel alignment that sits high or low in the window. These are solvable before dispatch. Once the watch ships, they are not.
